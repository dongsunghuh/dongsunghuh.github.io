Research

From continuous learning to discrete structure

Algebraic structure discovery

Learning the rule,
not just the examples.

Algebraic rules describe how operations compose. Can neural representations recover those rules from observations, rather than memorize a table?

My work studies group discovery through unitary representations and differentiable measures of algebraic complexity. A broader question drives my research: when do learning objectives favor computational rules that generalize beyond the observed examples?

Recent work →

Learning dynamics

The geometry of learning

How does parameter geometry shape optimization? I study learning dynamics, curvature corrections, and the sensitivity of neural predictions.

Curvature-corrected dynamics →

Neuroscience & control

Computation in dynamical systems

My neuroscience work investigates training spiking networks and the mathematical principles underlying movement, memory, and neural computation.

Spiking neural networks →

Looking ahead. I aim to understand when learned computational rules can be identified from finite evidence, discovered by practical training procedures, and composed reliably under finite precision and noise.

Background & mentoring

Physics, learning, and neural computation

A path through physics and neuroscience

I am a Visiting Scientist at Centaur AI Institute and an Affiliate at the NSF Institute for Artificial Intelligence and Fundamental Interactions (IAIFI). Previously, I was a Senior Research Scientist at IBM Research (2025–2026) and a Research Staff Member at the MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab (2018–2025).

I held research positions at the Qualcomm Institute, the Salk Institute with Terrence Sejnowski, and the Gatsby Computational Neuroscience Unit at UCL with Peter Dayan and Peter Latham.

I received my Ph.D. in Physics and M.S. in Neuroscience from UC San Diego, and bachelor's degrees in Physics and Brain and Cognitive Sciences from MIT.
